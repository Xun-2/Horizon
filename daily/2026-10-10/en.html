Budget-Based Fair Sharing for GPU Clusters

Background

Ai2 operates thousands of H100, B200, and B300 GPUs for roughly 150 researchers, while submitted demand is typically two to three times available capacity. Its priority-based scheduler encouraged universal HIGH-priority claims, GPU squatting, and long non-preemptible jobs that made maintenance dependent on negotiations with users.

Solution

Ai2 replaced project-specific GPU monopolies with hierarchical GPU-time budgets, letting managers allocate proportional shares while allowing unused capacity to flow to other teams. A fair-share scheduler compares actual occupancy with allocations over a seven-day sliding window; funded work receives preemption protection, while unallocated work runs free of budget charges but can be interrupted immediately. Jobs also declare a minimum useful runtime, capped at eight hours, after which resumable work may be preempted and requeued so allocations can converge and unhealthy hosts can drain automatically. Ai2 reports that during a 30-day test, teams received 98% of owed GPU hours, occupancy remained at 98%, debug-job p90 wait time fell from two hours to 30 seconds, and repairs requiring human intervention declined 74%. The rollout nevertheless required live education and new allocation visualizations, while time-slicing harmed stateful interactive sessions and may fragment capacity enough to delay the largest jobs—issues Ai2 is addressing through restorable CPU development sessions and further simulation.

Takeaway

The author’s central claim is that pricing every protected GPU hour against a transparent organizational budget aligns user incentives better than cost-free priorities, while preemptible time-slicing preserves occupancy and makes scarce capacity meaningfully shareable.
