Summary

The article presents ideas for building custom decision models rather than relying solely on general-purpose AI systems. The discussion centers on zero-shot classification, embeddings, SQLite-vec, CPU-based training, and lightweight alternatives for practical applications. It suggests that focused models may offer useful control or efficiency, although the available information does not establish a major technical breakthrough or a clear advantage over existing classifiers and large language models.

Background

A decision model returns calibrated probabilities across a defined set of allowed answers, rather than generating unrestricted text. This differs from using structured output with a language model, which constrains the format of its response but does not itself make the model a calibrated classifier.

Community Discussion

Commenters described building projects that classify inputs as categories, questions, or answers using SQLite-vec and small Qwen embedding models, while others pointed to Jev-like clones and the CPU-friendly Jeffy classifier. Discussion questioned whether algorithmic and data advantages justify billion-dollar valuations, requested concrete examples of useful decision-model applications, and argued that conventional classifiers or inexpensive large language models may already cover many zero-shot classification needs.
