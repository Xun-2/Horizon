Background

“Math 1.0” describes traditional mathematics as operating under proof scarcity: solving an open problem and establishing its correctness were difficult, uncommon achievements that typically required substantial human expertise. The “Math 2.0” framing considers how AI-generated solutions could change that assumption and reshape mathematical research and proof development. citetool-1-1

Impact

For mathematicians, AI proof systems could make some solutions easier to obtain while making human understanding, exposition, and the value of the research process more contested.

Community discussion

Commenters disagreed over whether understanding a mechanism is necessary when an AI-generated result works, with drug-discovery and medical examples used to argue that effective treatments are not always fully understood. Other commenters emphasized that Tao’s concern may apply specifically to pure mathematics, where failed attempts and the path to a proof can be as valuable as the final result, and cited reports that AI-generated solutions had disrupted ongoing research and grant plans; the available comments are partly truncated and do not establish how representative these experiences are.
